The problem

The choice is made once a year, and usually blind.

A smallholder farmer in the North-West picks a crop in May and finds out in October whether it was the right call. By then the land, the seed and the season are spent.

Prices are heard, not known

What a crop fetched last year in one market becomes the basis for this year's decision across a whole village. Nobody is comparing the actual regional figures.

Cost is left out of the sum

A crop with the best price can still be the worst choice once seed, fertiliser and labour are counted. The number that matters is what is left after the bill.

The data exists out of reach

NAERLS publishes yields, costs and market prices every year. It sits in a PDF report, in English, in a format built for institutions rather than for the person holding the hoe.

Evidence

One source, named, and checkable.

Every price, yield and cost on this site comes from the National Agricultural Extension and Research Liaison Services survey of the 2024 wet season, using North-West values. Market prices are July 2024.

We do not model, adjust or smooth the figures. If the survey has no comparable North-West price for a crop, the site prints Unavailable.
