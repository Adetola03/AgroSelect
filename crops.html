Maize

The region’s staple cereal. Reliable demand, but the highest input bill on this list.

Priced in the survey

At a glance

  • Indicative return₦1,157,018/ha
  • PlantingMay–June
  • HarvestAbout 3–4 months

Growing & risk

Planting: May–June

Harvest: About 3–4 months

Water: Rain-fed

Major risks: Fall armyworm, stem borers, drought or dry spells, and flooding.

Actual timing varies with rainfall, irrigation, variety and local growing conditions.

How the estimate was worked out

Revenue = 2.50 MT/ha (2,500 kg) × ₦858/kg ≈ ₦2,147,684/ha.
Return = ₦2,147,684 − ₦990,666 ≈ ₦1,157,018/ha.

Worth knowing: Widely grown across all seven states, so seed, agro-chemicals and buyers are easy to find.

Evidence

Where these figures come from.

Yield and production-cost figures are taken from the National Agricultural Extension and Research Liaison Services (NAERLS) Agricultural Performance Survey of the 2024 Wet Season in Nigeria, using the values reported for the North-West zone.

Market prices are the North-West values from the same report, recorded in July 2024. Where the report carries no comparable North-West price for a crop, AgroSelect shows Unavailable rather than substituting a national average or an estimate.

Region covered

Jigawa, Kaduna, Kano, Katsina, Kebbi, Sokoto and Zamfara.

How each figure is derived

  • Market price and expected yield and production cost are reported directly by the survey.
  • Indicative revenue = expected yield converted to kilograms, multiplied by the market price per kilogram.
  • Indicative return = indicative revenue minus production cost.

Nothing is modelled, adjusted for inflation or smoothed. Small differences between the published totals and a hand calculation come from rounding in the source report.

Important: these figures are indicative estimates for planning and comparison. Actual farm results differ, sometimes widely, because prices, yields, input costs, weather and market conditions all change between one season and the next.
