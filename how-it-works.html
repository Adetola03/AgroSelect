No black box. Here is the whole method.

You are being asked to weigh a year's income against these numbers. You are entitled to see exactly where each one comes from and what was done to it.

The process

Four steps, about a minute.

You describe your farm

State, area in hectares, and the budget you have before harvest. The state keeps the comparison inside the region the data covers; the area and budget are what everything else is scaled against.

You pick the crops in play

Any of the eight crops the survey covers. Two is enough for a comparison; all eight works just as well. The tool never adds a crop you did not choose or drop one you did.

We scale the published figures

Each crop's per-hectare cost, revenue and return — as published — is multiplied by your area. Nothing is re-derived, so the site always agrees with the source report.

We rank and check against your budget

Crops are ordered by total indicative return, then each is checked against what you can spend. Anything you cannot afford is flagged, with the area your budget would actually cover.

The arithmetic

Every calculation on this site, in full.

There are only four. None of them is clever, and that is deliberate — you should be able to check any figure on this site with a calculator.

1. Expected harvest

yield in MT/ha × 1,000 × your hectares = kilograms

Example: maize at 2.50 MT/ha on 2 ha → 2.50 × 1,000 × 2 = 5,000 kg.

2. Indicative revenue

published revenue per hectare × your hectares = revenue

The per-hectare revenue is itself the survey's yield multiplied by the survey's July 2024 North-West price. Where there is no comparable price, there is no revenue, and the site says Unavailable.

3. Indicative return

revenue − production cost = return

This is the figure crops are ranked by. It is what is left after the production bill — not profit, because it does not carry your land, your transport, your storage losses or your loan interest.

4. Return per ₦1 spent

return ÷ production cost = return per naira

Included because the biggest total return is often the one you cannot afford. This shows which crop works a limited budget hardest, which is a different question — and frequently a more useful one.

The inputs

Limits

What this comparison cannot do.

A tool that hides its limits is worse than no tool. These are the ones that matter most.

It cannot see your land

Soil, slope, drainage and how the plot has been farmed before will move your yield well away from a regional average. Your soil notes are carried through to the results, but they change no figure.

It cannot predict the market

Prices are from July 2024. By the time you harvest, they will have moved. A crop can look strong here and sell poorly, and the reverse.

It cannot price your risk

Rice shows the largest return in the dataset and also depends on water control you may not have. The comparison ranks return, not the chance of getting it.

It uses regional averages

One set of figures covers all seven states. Conditions in Kebbi and Kaduna are not identical, and the survey figures do not separate them.

It has gaps, and says so

Soybean and sesame have no comparable North-West price in the survey, so they cannot be ranked. They still appear, with their costs and yields.

Return is not profit

Land, transport, storage, post-harvest loss and loan interest are all outside these figures. Your real margin will be lower than the return shown.

Your data

Nothing you type leaves your phone.

AgroSelect has no account, no login and no server that receives your answers. The figures you enter are held in your browser tab so you can move between the questions and the results, and they are cleared when you close it.

Nothing is sold, shared or used to build a profile of you. There is nothing to delete, because there is nothing kept.
